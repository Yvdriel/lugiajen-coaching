# Youth Adaptation Guide — Modifying the Torres / del Moral System for Athletes Under 18

## Source
Sports science research on LTAD (Long-Term Athlete Development), motor learning in children, and overtraining prevention — applied to the specific training structures from the Karate Classroom methodology.

---

## 1. WHY THE ADULT MODEL CANNOT BE APPLIED DIRECTLY

The Torres/del Moral system was designed for Ariel Torres — an elite adult professional training 15+ hours/week with years of technical foundation, full physical maturity, and professional recovery support. Directly applying this to a 10-year-old would be:

- **Physically dangerous:** Growing bones and joints (especially during peak height velocity, ages 11-14) are vulnerable to overuse injuries from high-volume repetitive training
- **Technically counterproductive:** Children under 10 learn better from part practice, but the attention and self-correction capacity needed for ×10 rep sets isn't developmentally present
- **Psychologically harmful:** Grinding 45-60 minutes on one kata destroys intrinsic motivation in young athletes; burnout and dropout are the primary risks

**However:** The PRINCIPLES are universally valid. What changes is the dosing, not the method.

## 2. ADAPTATION BY AGE GROUP

### U12 (Ages 9-11) — "Learn to Train" Stage

**Training frequency reality:** 6-8 hours/week total, typically 1 competition-specific session

**Session structure adaptation:**

| Original Part | U12 Adaptation | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1: Warm-up | Game-based warm-up with movement challenges | 10-15 min |
| Part 2: Gym/S&C | Bodyweight movement skills: jumps, rolls, crawling, balance games. NO external weights. | 10-15 min |
| Part 3: Kihon | Focused kihon with variety and partner work | 10-15 min |
| Part 4: Kata Review | Review 1-2 katas lightly, partner observation games | 10-15 min |
| Part 5: Technical Kata | ONE kata, broken into sections, max 20-25 min | 20-25 min |
| Part 6: Kata Workout | Simplified, game-like format | 10-15 min |

**Technical Kata Training (Part 5) — U12 modifications:**
```
Break kata into 3-4 quarters
Each quarter: ×3-5 reps (NOT 10)
Rest: 45-60 seconds between reps (NOT 30)
Recovery between sections: 3 minutes
Include variety: "Show me with no hands" / "Do it super slow motion" / "Do it as a mirror image"

Maximum VLI targets:
  Volume: 12-16 per session
  Load: 4-5 maximum
  Intensity: 1-2.5/5 (quarter sections, generous rest)
```

**Kata Workout (Part 6) — U12 modifications:**
- Quarter Kata: 1 round only, 2 katas (not 3)
- EMOM: 5-6 rounds maximum, generous section size
- Beginning Blast: 3-4 reps in 8 minutes
- **NO** Burpee Kata, Leg Day Kata, Circuit Kata, or Vest training
- **ADD** game-based alternatives: "Kata tag" (perform section correctly to unfreeze teammate), "Judge game" (athletes score each other and explain why), "Challenge ladder" (beat your own best focus score)

**Critical coach behavior for U12:**
- Use external focus cues: "Push the floor away" not "Bend your knees"
- Feedback after EVERY rep (100% frequency with external focus = optimal per Wulf research)
- Maximum 1 correction per rep
- Praise effort and specific improvement, never talent: "Your stance was deeper that time because you pushed hard" not "You're so good at stances"
- Attention span: change activity every 10-15 minutes
- End every session on a high note (something the athlete does well)

**Periodization for U12:**
The 6-week cycle is too aggressive. Use a simplified 3-week competition prep:
- Week 1: Normal training, introduce competition kata focus
- Week 2: Slightly more competition simulation, practice bowing in/out, walking to the mat
- Week 3 (competition): Light training early in week, focus on mental prep and confidence

### U14 (Ages 12-14) — "Train to Train" Stage

**WARNING: Peak Height Velocity (PHV).** This age group is the highest injury risk due to rapid growth. Tendons and ligaments lag behind bone growth. Watch for: knee pain, heel pain (Sever's disease), hip/back complaints. Reduce volume IMMEDIATELY if growth-related pain appears.

**Training frequency reality:** ~10 hours/week, possibly 2 competition-specific sessions

**Technical Kata Training — U14 modifications:**
```
Break kata into quarters
Each quarter: ×5-7 reps
Rest: 30-45 seconds between reps
Recovery between sections: 2-3 minutes
Begin introducing half-kata work (×1-2 per session, building to ×3)

Maximum VLI targets:
  Volume: 16-24 per session
  Load: 6-7 maximum
  Intensity: Up to 3.5/5
```

**Kata Workout — U14 modifications:**
- Quarter Kata: 2 rounds, 3 katas
- EMOM: 7-8 rounds
- Beginning Blast: 4-5 reps in 8 minutes
- Burpee Kata: Allowed with reduced burpees (3 instead of 5)
- Leg Day Kata: Allowed
- Upper Body Kata: Allowed
- Circuit Kata: Modified version — 2-3 rounds, lighter resistance, can substitute exercises (jump rope for battle rope, bodyweight squats for box jumps)
- **NO** vest training before age 14, and only with parental/medical clearance after 14

**Periodization for U14:**
Can follow a 4-5 week competition prep cycle:
- Weeks 1-2: Building (moderate volume, low-moderate intensity)
- Week 3: Transmutation (volume decreasing, intensity increasing)
- Week 4: Taper (low volume, high quality)
- Week 5 (if available): Competition week

### U18 (Ages 15-17) — "Train to Compete" Stage

**Training frequency targets:** 8-12 hours/week, 3-5 sessions

**Technical Kata Training — U18 modifications:**
```
Can follow adult model with 20-30% reduction in maximum volume
Each quarter: ×5-8 reps (week 1 loading: ×7 instead of ×10)
Rest: 30 seconds (adult standard)
Half katas: Full integration, ×3 per session
Full katas: Can begin integrating post-section (like Post-Istanbul format)

Maximum VLI targets:
  Volume: 20-30 per session
  Load: 8-9 maximum
  Intensity: Full spectrum (1-5/5)
```

**Kata Workout — U18:**
- All basic and advanced workouts available
- Circuit Kata: Full protocol, 3-5 rounds
- Vest training: Available from age 15+ with these modifications:
  - Vest weight: 8-10% of bodyweight (lower end of the 10-15% range)
  - Maximum 2× per week in designated weeks
  - Always supervised
  - Discontinue immediately if any joint pain

**Periodization for U18:**
Can follow the full 6-week model with minor adjustments:
- Week 1 max load: 8 (not 10)
- Circuit: 4 rounds max (not 5)
- Same taper and peaking structure as adult model

---

### U21 (Ages 18-20) — Full Adult Model, No Reductions

U21 athletes follow the Torres/del Moral system 1:1 as designed for adult athletes. No reductions in volume, load, intensity, or any other parameter. All workout formats, all periodization phases, and all training structures apply exactly as described in the VLI Reference Manual and Session Architecture documents.

- Maximum load per session: 10 (full adult ceiling)
- Full intensity spectrum (1-5/5)
- Full volume targets (40+ in loading phase for Category A kata)
- All workout formats including Circuit Kata and Weight Vest training
- Full 6-week periodization model without modification

## 3. THE ONCE-PER-WEEK COACHING PROBLEM

**The core challenge:** Most youth athletes can only attend competition-specific coaching 1× per week. The Torres/del Moral system assumes 5 days/week.

### The Bridge Protocol

**Day 1 (Coached Session):**
Full session following adapted 6-part structure. Coach records video of technical kata work. Coach provides maximum 3 priority corrections with external focus cues. Athlete writes corrections in training journal immediately post-session.

**Day 2 (Home — Active Review, 15-20 min):**
Watch video of own session (5 min). Review corrections from journal. Visualization: mentally perform kata 3× with eyes closed, focusing on corrections (10 min). Light stretching.

**Day 3 (Home — Solo Practice, 20-30 min):**
Warm-up: 1 full kata at 50% (5 min). Main work: weakest quarter section × 3-5 reps, focusing on corrections from Day 1 (15 min). Cool-down: strongest kata at 70% for confidence (5 min).

**Day 5 (Home — Model Study + Practice, 20-30 min):**
Watch expert model video of same kata — 2-3 performances (5 min). Visualization: 3× mental rehearsal (5 min). Practice: beginning section of competition kata × 3-5 (10 min). Practice: ending section × 3-5 (5 min).

**Day 7 (Regular Dojo Session):**
Even if the regular dojo session isn't competition-focused, the athlete can apply corrections and perform their competition kata during open practice time.

### Why This Works (Sports Science Basis)
- **Spacing effect:** 3+ practice exposures per week across different days produces superior retention versus one long session
- **Sleep consolidation:** Motor memories strengthen during sleep following practice — distributed practice gives more consolidation opportunities
- **Mental rehearsal:** 10 minutes, 3× per week is the optimal dose for motor skill enhancement through visualization
- **Combined action observation + motor imagery:** Watching video while imagining performing activates similar neural pathways to physical practice

### Minimum Effective Dose
Research suggests a minimum of 3 practice exposures per week (physical + mental combined) to maintain skill acquisition trajectory. Below this threshold, week-to-week progress becomes negligible as motor memory decays between sessions.

## 4. OVERTRAINING PREVENTION IN YOUTH

### General Rule: Training Hours Per Week ≤ Athlete's Age
A 10-year-old should not train more than 10 hours/week total (all sports combined). A 14-year-old should not exceed 14 hours/week.

### Warning Signs by Age Group

**U12:**
- Loss of enthusiasm for training (THE most important warning sign)
- Increased illness frequency
- Complaints of persistent fatigue
- Regression in skills that were previously solid
- Sleep disruption
- Avoidance behaviors (excuses to skip training)

**U14 (heightened risk during PHV):**
- All U12 signs PLUS:
- Growth plate pain (knees, heels, hips)
- Sudden coordination loss (normal during growth spurts — reduce volume, don't push through)
- Emotional volatility beyond normal adolescence
- Repeated minor injuries

**U18:**
- Performance plateau despite maintained training
- Chronic fatigue not resolved by normal rest
- Elevated resting heart rate
- Mood disturbances, irritability
- Loss of motivation
- Decreased appetite

### Recovery Protocol by Age

**U12 Active Recovery:**
- 1-2 complete rest days per week (non-negotiable)
- Light play/movement on recovery days (swimming, cycling, playground — NOT karate)
- 9-11 hours sleep per night
- No intensive training 2 days before competition

**U14 Active Recovery:**
- 1-2 complete rest days per week
- Mobility work and light movement on recovery days
- 8-10 hours sleep per night
- Recovery week every 4-5 weeks of training (see Recovery Week template from Ariel's actual cycle)

**U18/U21 Active Recovery:**
- 1 complete rest day per week minimum
- Active recovery sessions (mobility, foam rolling, light movement)
- 8-9 hours sleep per night
- Recovery week every 3-4 weeks of intense training
- Can follow Ariel's Recovery Training Week format with appropriate scaling

### Ariel's Recovery Week — Adapted for Youth

The Recovery Training Week template can serve as a youth recovery session model:
```
U14 version:
1. Touch all competition katas lightly: 1/3 (×1-2) per section, long rest
2. One kata for detail work: 1/3 (×2) per section, relaxed pace
3. Main kata: 1/4 (×2) with generous time limits (10 min per section)
4. Light workout: 1 round per kata, 2 katas only

Key principle: Time-boxed rather than rep-based. "You have 8-10 minutes for this section — do what you can, quality over quantity."
```

## 5. MOTIVATION AND ENGAGEMENT STRATEGIES BY AGE

### U12: Intrinsic Motivation Through Autonomy and Competence
- Let athletes choose which section to work on during workout time
- Use "challenge format": "Can you do this section 3 times without losing balance?"
- Peer teaching: "Show your partner how to do this transition"
- Visible progress tracking: sticker charts, small milestone celebrations
- Competition preparation as exciting adventure, not high-pressure test
- NEVER tie love/approval to performance outcomes

### U14: Identity and Social Connection
- Create team identity around the competition group
- Pair athletes for mutual video review
- Give older U14s responsibility for mentoring younger athletes
- Connect daily training to long-term dreams: "This section work is what Ariel Torres does every day"
- Begin involving athletes in their own goal-setting
- Acknowledge the difficulty of balancing school, social life, and training

### U18/U21: Self-Determination and Purpose
- Full involvement in training plan design
- Athlete leads their own video review sessions
- Process goal ownership: athlete sets and tracks their own weekly targets
- Connect training to competition strategy: "We're working on this section because it's where you lost the point at regionals"
- Build identity as a serious athlete making conscious sacrifices
- Peer accountability structures

## 6. SECTION SIZE SELECTION FOR DIFFERENT AGES

The Torres system uses quarters (1/4) as the standard section size. For youth athletes, section size affects both difficulty and engagement:

| Age Group | Recommended Section Sizes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| U12 (9-11) | 1/4 preferred, 1/3 for simple katas | Smaller sections = more manageable chunks, lower intensity |
| U14 (12-14) | 1/4 standard, begin 1/3 and occasional 1/2 | Can handle larger sections as proficiency grows |
| U18 (15-17) | Full range: 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, full | Same as adult model |

**Part practice vs. whole practice age effect:** Research (Giordano et al., 2015) shows children ages 6-9 learn motor skills better with part practice, while children ages 10-11 learn better with whole practice. This means:
- **Ages 9-10:** Use quarters extensively, occasionally combining into halves
- **Ages 11+:** Begin using the "attention approach" — perform the whole kata but hyper-focus on one section's corrections
- **Ages 14+:** Full progressive-part method (A → B → A+B → C → A+B+C)
