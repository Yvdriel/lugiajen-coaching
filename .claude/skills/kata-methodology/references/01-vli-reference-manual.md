# VLI Reference Manual — Torres / del Moral Kata Training System

## Source
Extracted and synthesized from Ariel Torres Gutierrez's "Karate Classroom" series (Episodes 1-6), real training cycles (Pre-comp Istanbul, Post-Istanbul/Pre-Nationals, Recovery Week), and cross-referenced with sports science research. This methodology originates from Jesus del Moral, coach of Sandra Sanchez (Olympic gold, 7x European champion) and Ariel Torres (Olympic bronze, 5x Pan American champion).

---

## 1. VOLUME — Total Repetitions

**Definition:** Volume = the total number of reps (resets) performed across all sections and formats in a training session's technical kata work.

### Counting Rules

| Training Element | How to Count Volume |
|---|---|
| 1/4 section × 1 rep | Volume = 1 |
| 1/3 section × 1 rep | Volume = 1 |
| Half kata × 1 rep | Volume = 1 |
| Full kata × 1 rep | Volume = 1 |

**Every individual performance of any section or full kata = 1 unit of volume.**

### Calculation Examples

**Example A — High Volume Week (EP.1, 6+ weeks out)**
- 4 quarters × 10 reps each = **Volume of 40**
- Load = 10 (see below)

**Example B — From EP.2 (first referenced week)**
- 4 quarters × 5 reps each = 20
- No half katas mentioned
- **Volume = 20**, Load = 5

**Example C — From EP.2 (second referenced week, ~5 weeks out)**
- 4 quarters × 5 reps each = 20
- First half kata × 3 reps = 3
- Second half kata × 3 reps = 3
- **Volume = 26**, Load = 8

**Example D — Pre-comp Istanbul (actual training)**
- Warm-up/Review Kururunfa: 1/3 × 1, 2/3 × 1, 3/3 × 1 = 3 per round × 5 rounds = **Volume 15**
- Tech Training Anan Dai: 1/4 (x3 + x8) = 11 per section × 4 sections = **Volume ~44**
- Workout (3 katas, quarter kata format, 2 rounds each): **Volume ~30**
- **Total session volume: ~89**

**Example E — Post-Istanbul / Pre-Nationals (actual training)**
- Warm-up Kururunfa: 3 sections × (3 reps + 2 full kata) = **Volume 15**
- Tech Anan Dai: 4 quarters × (4 reps + 1 full kata) = **Volume 20**
- Workout EMOM: 3 katas × 10 rounds = **Volume 30**
- **Total session volume: ~65**

**Example F — Recovery Week (actual training)**
- 5 kata review: 3 thirds × 2 reps × 5 katas = **Volume 30**
- Anan detail: 3 thirds × 3 reps = **Volume 9**
- Main kata: 3 quarters × 3 reps + half × 1 = **Volume 10**
- Workout: 3 katas × 2 rounds = **Volume ~6**
- **Total session volume: ~55** (lower intensity, recovery focus)

### Key Principle
**Far from competition → HIGH volume. Close to competition → LOW volume.**

Typical volume progression across a 6-week cycle:
- Week 1 (6 weeks out): Very high volume (40+ for main kata alone)
- Week 2-3: Moderate-high volume
- Week 4-5: Moderate volume, intensity rising
- Week 6 (competition): Very low volume (load of ~2)

## 2. LOAD — Full Kata Equivalents

**Definition:** Load = the total number of equivalent full katas completed when you add up all sections performed.

### Counting Rules

| Training Format | How to Calculate Load |
|---|---|
| All 4 quarters × N reps each | Load = N (you completed the full kata N times in pieces) |
| All 3 thirds × N reps each | Load = N |
| Both halves × N reps each | Load = N |
| Full kata × N reps | Load = N |
| Mixed: quarters × 5 + halves × 3 | Load = 5 + 3 = 8 |

**Load tracks how many times the COMPLETE kata has been performed (even if in pieces), giving a measure of total physical demand.**

### Load Thresholds and Limits

From EP.2: "Our body is able to produce 10 full katas at its best. Anything more than that, our body is going to be pushing the limit, we're risking injury, and we're really risking overtraining."

**Maximum sustainable load per kata per session: ~10**

### Load Periodization (from EP.2)
The load decreases progressively toward competition:
- 6+ weeks out: Load of 10 (building phase)
- 5 weeks out: Load of 8
- 4 weeks out: Load of 7
- 3 weeks out: Load of 6
- 2 weeks out: Load of 5
- Competition week: **Load of 2**

### Load from Real Training Cycles

| Training Cycle | Tech Kata Load | Workout Load | Context |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-comp Istanbul | ~11 (Anan Dai) | ~6 (3 katas w/ vest) | Final prep before major competition |
| Post-Istanbul/Pre-Nationals | ~9 (Anan Dai) | ~10 (EMOM, high volume) | Between competitions, building again |
| Recovery Week | ~3 (main kata) | ~2 (light workout) | Active recovery, all 5 katas touched lightly |

## 3. INTENSITY — Rest Time × Section Size

**Definition:** Intensity is determined by TWO factors: how long the rest periods are, and how large the sections being performed are.

### Rest Time Component

| Rest Duration | Intensity Level |
|---|---|
| > 30 seconds | Low to Moderate |
| ≤ 30 seconds | Moderate to High |
| No rest between reps | High |

### Section Size Component

| Section Size | Intensity Level |
|---|---|
| 1/4 kata (quarter) | Low intensity |
| 1/3 kata (third) | Moderate intensity |
| 1/2 kata (half) | High intensity |
| Full kata | High intensity |
| 1/5 or 1/6 | Very low (not recommended except for isolating specific techniques) |

### Combined Intensity Rating Scale (1-5)

The overall intensity of a session is rated on a 1-5 scale combining both factors:

- **1 (Very Low):** Quarter sections, 30+ sec rest, high reps — pure volume building
- **2 (Low-Moderate):** Quarter sections with moderate rest, few or no half/full katas — EP.2 "week 1" example
- **3 (Moderate):** Mix of quarters and thirds, 30 sec rest, some half katas
- **4 (Moderate-High):** Includes significant half kata work, 30 sec or less rest
- **4.5 (High):** Many half katas, shorter rest periods — EP.2 "week 2" example
- **5 (Maximum):** Full kata work, minimal rest, competition simulation

### Key Principle
**Far from competition → LOW intensity. Close to competition → HIGH intensity.**

This is the INVERSE of volume. As volume decreases, intensity increases. The relationship:

```
Week 1-2 (far out):   HIGH volume  +  LOW intensity   = Building/Loading phase
Week 3-4 (mid):       MED volume   +  MED intensity   = Transmutation phase
Week 5 (taper):       LOW volume   +  HIGH intensity   = Peaking phase
Week 6 (competition): VERY LOW vol +  MAX intensity    = Competition (load of 2)
```

## 4. VLI COMPARISON FRAMEWORK

When planning or reviewing training, always calculate and compare all three:

| Metric | What It Tells You | Danger Sign |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | Total training demand / repetitions | Too high close to competition = fatigue |
| Load | Physical stress (full kata equivalents) | Exceeding 10 per kata risks overtraining |
| Intensity | Quality demand per rep | Too high too early = burnout; too low close to comp = underprepared |

### Side-by-Side from EP.2

| | Week A (further from comp) | Week B (closer to comp) |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | 14 | 26 |
| Load | 10 | 8 |
| Intensity | 2/5 | 4-4.5/5 |
| Character | High reps, low stress per rep | Fewer reps, higher stress per rep |

**NOTE:** Volume went UP in Week B while load went DOWN — this is because the higher number of half-kata reps each count as 1 volume but contribute more to intensity than quarter-kata reps. The volume number alone doesn't tell the full story; it must be read alongside intensity and load.

## 5. APPLYING VLI TO TRAINING PLANNING

### Pre-Session Planning Checklist
1. How many weeks until the next competition?
2. Based on the periodization curve, what should today's VLI targets be?
3. Which kata is the main technical kata today?
4. What section size will I use? (determines base intensity)
5. What rest intervals will I use? (fine-tunes intensity)
6. How many reps per section? (sets volume)
7. Will I include half or full katas? (increases both load and intensity)

### Post-Session Review
After every training, calculate:
- Total Volume (sum all reps across all formats)
- Total Load (full kata equivalents for the main kata)
- Estimated Intensity (1-5 scale based on section sizes and rest)
- Compare to previous sessions and the periodization target

### Red Flags
- Load consistently above 10 → overtraining risk
- Volume not decreasing as competition approaches → insufficient taper
- Intensity staying low in final 2 weeks → athlete will be underprepared for competition effort
- Athlete cannot maintain quality in final reps → volume too high for current fitness level

## 6. SHOTOKAN-SPECIFIC VLI ADJUSTMENTS

**CRITICAL: See "07-Shotokan-Kata-Reference.md" for complete details.**

The Torres system uses longer Okinawan kata (~60+ movements) that split cleanly into quarters. Most Shotokan competition kata are shorter (24-48 movements). The key principle for adaptation is that **absolute movement count per section determines training demand, not the fraction of the kata.**

A 1/4 section of Gojushiho Sho (~60 movements) contains ~15 movements. A 1/3 section of Sochin (~40 movements) contains ~13 movements. The 1/3 section is NOT more intense — it is shorter in absolute terms. Therefore:

**Category A kata** (Gojushiho Sho/Dai, Kanku Sho) — split to 1/4. Follow VLI calculations as described above. These kata are long enough that their sections match Torres system assumptions directly.

**Category B kata** (Kanku Dai, Sochin, Unsu, Enpi, Gankaku) — minimum split 1/3. Sections contain fewer movements than Torres quarter sections. **Increase rep count by ~25%** compared to quarter-based targets to achieve equivalent training stimulus. Rest periods stay at Torres standard (30 sec). No intensity adjustment needed — section intensity is the same or lower, not higher. (Exception: Kanku Dai at ~65 movements is long enough that its 1/3 sections do not need the volume increase — treat like Category A for VLI purposes.)

**Category C kata** (Heian series) — minimum split 1/2. Sections are very short (~10-14 movements). **Increase rep count by ~50%** compared to quarter-based targets. Rest stays at 30 seconds. These kata can be used across all training phases with appropriate volume adjustment, not just for warm-up/review.

Consult the Shotokan Kata Reference for adapted VLI targets per phase, workout format compatibility, and strategic kata selection.

## 7. SPECIAL NOTES FOR YOUTH APPLICATION

The VLI system was designed for elite adult athletes training 5 days/week. For youth athletes:

**U12 (ages 9-11):**
- Maximum load per session: 4-5 (not 10)
- Prefer 1/4 sections (lower intensity) with longer rest (45-60 sec)
- Intensity should rarely exceed 3/5
- Total session volume for main kata: 12-16

**U14 (ages 12-14):**
- Maximum load per session: 6-7
- Can begin introducing 1/3 sections and occasional half kata
- Intensity can reach 3.5/5
- Total session volume for main kata: 16-24

**U18 (ages 15-17):**
- Can follow the adult model with 20-30% reduction in maximum volume
- Maximum load per session: 8-9
- Full intensity spectrum available
- Total session volume for main kata: 20-30+

**U21 (ages 18-20):**
- Follow the adult Torres/del Moral model 1:1. No reductions.
- Maximum load per session: 10 (full adult ceiling)
- Full intensity spectrum, full volume targets, full periodization model
- All workout formats available without modification

**For athletes training only 1x/week in competition-specific format:**
- Prioritize moderate volume, moderate intensity (maximize learning per session)
- Load of 4-6 is realistic in a single coached session
- Supplement with home practice at lower intensity (1/4 sections, self-paced)
