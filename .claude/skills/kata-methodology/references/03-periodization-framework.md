# Periodization Framework — Torres / del Moral System

## Source
Synthesized from Karate Classroom Episodes 1-6, three real training cycles from Ariel Torres, and sports science periodization principles. This document maps the complete competition preparation timeline.

---

## 1. THE 6-WEEK COMPETITION PREPARATION MODEL

The Torres/del Moral system uses a **6-week minimum preparation cycle** before any target competition. Each week has a distinct character, and multiple training modalities taper or ramp according to their own schedules.

### Overview Table

| Week | Distance from Comp | Volume | Load | Intensity | Circuit Rounds | Vest | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 weeks out | VERY HIGH | 10 | 1-2/5 | 5 | No | Loading / Building |
| 2 | 5 weeks out | HIGH | 8 | 3-4/5 | 5 | No | Building with intensity |
| 3 | 4 weeks out | MODERATE-HIGH | 7 | 3.5/5 | 4 | 3× | Transmutation + vest |
| 4 | 3 weeks out | MODERATE | 6 | 4/5 | 4 | 2× | Peak training load |
| 5 | 2 weeks out | LOW | 5 | 4.5/5 | 3 | No | Taper |
| 6 | Competition week | VERY LOW | 2 | 5/5 | 0 | No | Peak / Realization |

### The Inverse Relationship (Central Principle)

```
VOLUME:    ████████████████░░░░░░░░░░░░  (decreases →)
INTENSITY: ░░░░░░░░░░░░████████████████  (increases →)
           Week 1  →  →  →  →  →  Week 6

LOAD:      ████████████████████░░░░░░░░  (decreases →)
           10    8    7    6    5    2
```

## 2. WEEK-BY-WEEK BREAKDOWN

### Week 1 — Loading Phase (6 weeks out)
**Goal:** Build physical foundation, establish high training tolerance, identify technical priorities.

**Technical Kata Training (Part 5):**
- Kata in quarters × 10 reps each, 30 sec rest between reps
- 2-3 min recovery between quarters
- One kata per day, 5 different katas across 5 days
- VLI: Volume ~40, Load 10, Intensity 2/5
- **This extreme volume is done for ONE WEEK ONLY**

**Kata Workout (Part 6):**
- Quarter kata: 3 rounds per kata, 3 katas
- EMOM: 10 rounds per kata
- Beginning blast: 6 reps per kata

**Circuit:** 5 rounds (Thursday)

**Vest:** Not used

**S&C Focus:** Hypertrophy / maximal strength development

---

### Week 2 — Building with Intensity (5 weeks out)
**Goal:** Begin introducing half-kata work, increase intensity while maintaining substantial volume.

**Technical Kata Training:**
- Quarters × 5 reps + half katas × 3 reps (as per EP.2)
- 30 sec rest between quarter reps, 1 min between half katas, 3 min full recovery
- VLI: Volume ~26, Load 8, Intensity 4-4.5/5

**Kata Workout:** Same rotation as Week 1 with slight reduction (EMOM: 9 rounds, Beginning blast: 5 reps)

**Circuit:** 5 rounds

**Vest:** Not used

---

### Week 3 — Transmutation + Vest Introduction (4 weeks out)
**Goal:** Convert general fitness into kata-specific power. Introduce contrast training.

**Technical Kata Training:**
- Volume decreasing, section quality increasing
- More half and full kata integration
- Load ~7

**Kata Workout:** Vest workouts replace 3 standard workouts this week

**Circuit:** 4 rounds

**Vest:** 3 sessions this week (always at END of training, 20-40 min each)
- Vest protocol: 2 quarter sections × (3 with vest + 2 without) × 3 cycles + full kata contrast

---

### Week 4 — Peak Training Load (3 weeks out)
**Goal:** Highest combined training stress. The "make or break" week.

**Technical Kata Training:**
- Moderate volume with high technical demand
- Load ~6
- Full competition kata runs beginning to appear

**Kata Workout:** Vest workouts 2 sessions this week

**Circuit:** 4 rounds

**Vest:** 2 sessions

---

### Week 5 — Taper (2 weeks out)
**Goal:** Reduce volume, sharpen intensity, allow physical supercompensation.

**Technical Kata Training:**
- Low volume, high quality
- Load ~5
- Focus on competition simulation runs
- Every rep at near-competition intensity

**Kata Workout:** Reduced rounds (Quarter kata: 1 round per kata)

**Circuit:** 3 rounds (lightest circuit week)

**Vest:** Not used (taper)

---

### Week 6 — Competition Week
**Goal:** Peak performance. Minimal training stress. Maximum readiness.

**Technical Kata Training:**
- Load of 2 (typically 1-2 full kata runs at competition intensity)
- Focus on feel, timing, breathing
- "Different ways of doing the kata" (likely includes visualization, walk-throughs, mental rehearsal)

**Kata Workout:** Minimal or none

**Circuit:** None

**Vest:** None

## 3. REAL TRAINING CYCLE ANALYSIS

### Pre-comp Istanbul — Final Preparation

This represents a late-stage (likely Week 4-5) training session:

```
PART 4: Kururunfa Review
  1/3 (×1) → 10' rest → 2/3 (×1) → 10' rest → 3/3 (×1) → 30' rest
  × 5 ROUNDS
  Volume: 15 | Character: Gentle, systematic review

PART 5: Anan Dai Technical Training
  1/4 (×3) "con math" 10' rest
  + 1/4 (×8) 30' rest + 2 min
  → repeat for 2/4, 3/4, 4/4
  Volume: ~44 | Load: ~11 | Character: HIGH volume technical drilling
  NOTE: "con math" = with mathematical/measured corrections (specific technical focus)

PART 6: Quarter Kata Workout WITH VEST
  Ohandai 3/4 | Suparinpei 2/3 | Anan 3/4
  Pattern: 50%, 100%, 50%, 100%, 100%
  2 rounds per kata
  Character: Contrast training (vest) on key sections of secondary katas
```

**Analysis:** This is an intensive session — the technical load on Anan Dai is very high (×11 reps at ×8 per section suggests deep drilling of corrections). The vest workout at the end adds physical stress. This would be a Week 3-4 session in the 6-week model.

---

### Post-Istanbul / Between Competitions / Before Nationals — Rebuilding

This represents a "between competitions" phase where the athlete is rebuilding after one peak and preparing for the next:

```
PART 4: Kururunfa Warm-up (Extended)
  1/3 (×3) 10' rest + 1 min + Full kata (×2) 1 min rest between
  2/3 (×3) = same
  3/3 (×3) = same
  Volume: 15 | Load: ~9 (3 sections × 3 + 6 full katas)
  Character: Heavy review — rebuilding competition readiness on secondary kata

PART 5: Anan Dai Technical
  1/4 (×4) 30' + 2 min + 1× Full kata, 3 min rest
  → repeat for each quarter
  Volume: 20 | Load: 9 (4 from quarters + 4 full katas + 1 final)
  Character: Moderate volume with full-kata integration checks

PART 6: EMOM Workout — Half Kata Sections Only
  10 rounds each:
  - Ohandai
  - Anan (3 min rest after completing each kata)
  - Suparinpei
  Volume: 30 | Character: High endurance demand, EMOM on half-katas = very intense
```

**Analysis:** This is a high-total-volume session. The warm-up alone has heavy load (full katas after each section). This looks like a Week 1-2 type session after a recovery period — rebuilding base before the next competition peak.

**Key structural difference from pre-comp:** Full katas are integrated AFTER each quarter section (not just at the end), and the EMOM uses HALF sections (not quarters) — both increasing intensity and load compared to base building.

---

### Recovery Training Week

This represents active recovery after a competition or heavy training block:

```
PART 4: 5-Kata Light Review
  All 5 katas: 1/3 (×2) 10' rest → through 3/3
  30 sec rest between katas → move to next
  Volume: 30 (5 katas × 6 reps) | Character: Light touch on everything

PART 5a: Anan Detail Work
  1/3 (×3) 30' + 2 min → through 3/3
  Volume: 9 | Character: Moderate detail work, not pushing load

PART 5b: Main Kata (reduced)
  1/4 (×3) → 3/4 + 1/2 (×1)
  8 min per section
  Volume: ~10 | Load: ~3-4 | Character: Time-boxed, light load

PART 6: Light Quarter Kata Workout
  Kururunfa 7/3, Awandai 1/3, Ohandai 3/4
  2 rounds each, 2 min rest
  Volume: ~6 | Character: Minimal workout, maintaining movement patterns
```

**Analysis:** Total session volume (~55) is substantially lower than pre-comp (~89) or between-comp (~65). Every element is scaled down. The 8-minute time-boxing per section is notable — it limits total output by capping time rather than prescribing reps, naturally preventing overexertion.

**Key recovery indicators:**
- Low rep counts (×2-3 instead of ×5-10)
- All 5 katas touched but none pushed hard
- Time-boxed rather than rep-based format for main kata
- Light workout (2 rounds, not 3)

## 4. COMPETITION SPACING AND BACK-TO-BACK CYCLES

The Post-Istanbul training reveals how Ariel handles competitions that are close together:

```
Competition A (Istanbul) → Recovery → Rebuild (1-2 weeks) → Taper → Competition B (Nationals)
```

When competitions are spaced 3-4 weeks apart:
- Week 1 post-comp: Recovery training (reduced everything)
- Week 2: Between-comp format (rebuild with moderate-high volume)
- Week 3: Taper (intensity up, volume down)
- Week 4: Competition

When competitions are spaced 6+ weeks apart:
- Full 6-week cycle as described above

## 5. APPLYING THE 6-WEEK MODEL TO DIFFERENT ATHLETE LEVELS

### Elite (Ariel's level: 15+ hrs/week, 5 days)
Follow the model as described. Each day is a separate main kata.

### Advanced Club / National Level (10-14 hrs/week, 4-5 days)
- Same structure but reduce reps per section by 30-40%
- Week 1 loading: ×7 instead of ×10 per quarter
- Maximum load per session: 7-8 instead of 10
- Circuit: 3-4 rounds instead of 5

### Developing Competitor (6-10 hrs/week, 3-4 days)
- Focus 3 competition katas instead of 5
- Week 1 loading: ×5 per quarter
- Maximum load per session: 5-6
- Circuit: 2-3 rounds, simplified (can substitute exercises)
- May need to combine Part 4 and Part 5 on some days

### Youth / Limited Access (3-6 hrs/week, 1-2 competition sessions)
- Focus 2-3 competition katas
- Technical training: ×3-5 per quarter, emphasize quality
- Maximum load: 3-5 per session
- Workout: 1 round per kata, basic formats only
- Circuit: simplified version 1-2 rounds
- No vest training for U14 and below
- Longer rest periods (45-60 sec instead of 30)

### Critical Note for Coaches
The periodization timing (6-week cycle) remains valid across all levels. What changes is the ABSOLUTE volume and load within each week, not the progression pattern. An U12 athlete still tapers, still peaks, still reduces volume and increases intensity toward competition — just at proportionally lower absolute numbers.

## 6. KATA ROTATION AND SECTION SELECTION

### 5-Kata Rotation Across 5 Days (from EP.5)
| Day | Main Technical Kata (Part 5) | Workout Katas (Part 6) |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Kata A | B, C, D (one section each) |
| Tuesday | Kata B | A, C, E (one section each) |
| Wednesday | Kata C | A, D, E (one section each) |
| Thursday | Circuit (rotates through A-E) | Post-circuit: 2 best katas, half kata format |
| Friday | Competition rounds (all katas) | — |

### Section Selection Strategy
**For workouts (Part 6):**
- Far from competition: Choose WEAKEST sections (improvement focus)
- Close to competition: Choose STRONGEST sections (confidence building) or opening/closing sections (first/last impression for judges)

**For technical training (Part 5):**
- Always work ALL sections systematically
- Spend extra reps on problem areas within the structure
- "Con math" approach: specific, measured corrections per section

### Pre-Planning Requirement (from EP.4)
"It's very important that the day before the training, you already know what section of the kata you're going to practice."

Coaches should plan the entire training week in advance:
- Which kata is the main technical focus each day
- Which sections of each workout kata to target
- Which workout format to use each day
- VLI targets for the week based on periodization phase
