# Shotokan Kata Reference — Splitting Options & Training Implications

## Source
Coach-provided splitting data for Shotokan competition kata, cross-referenced with the Torres/del Moral VLI system and workout catalog. This document addresses the critical adaptation needed when applying a methodology designed for longer Okinawan kata (Anan Dai, Suparinpei, Kururunfa, Ohandai, Awandai) to shorter Shotokan forms.

---

## 1. THE SHOTOKAN LENGTH PROBLEM

The Torres/del Moral system is built around kata that are significantly longer than most Shotokan forms. Ariel's competition kata (Anan Dai, Suparinpei, Kururunfa, Ohandai, Awandai) are all Okinawan forms with 40-65+ movements that split cleanly into quarters. Most Shotokan competition kata have 24-48 movements, and several cannot be split smaller than thirds.

**The key insight: absolute movement count per section, not the fraction, determines training demand.**

A 1/4 section of Gojushiho Sho (~60 movements) contains approximately 15 movements. A 1/3 section of Sochin (~40 movements) contains approximately 13 movements. The 1/3 section is NOT more intense — it is actually shorter in absolute terms. A full Sochin is less total physical work than a full Gojushiho Sho.

**This has three major consequences:**

1. **Shorter kata need MORE reps to match training effect.** Because each section contains fewer movements and each full kata equivalent represents less total work, Category B and C kata require increased rep counts to achieve equivalent training stimulus. The old assumption that 1/3 sections are "more intense" and therefore need fewer reps was incorrect — the opposite is true.

2. **Some workout formats need structural adaptation.** Quarter Kata (50-100-50-100-100) requires 1/4 sections. For kata that only split to 1/3, this workout must use 1/3 sections with adjusted rep patterns. Circuit Kata Training requires stations matching the number of available sections (3 for Category B, 2 for Category C).

3. **Rest periods stay at Torres baseline.** Because sections are the same length or shorter than Torres quarter sections, there is no need for extended rest. Standard 30-second rest between reps applies across all categories.

### Splitting Flexibility Categories

**Category A — Full Flexibility (can split to 1/4):**
Gojushiho Sho, Gojushiho Dai, Kanku Sho

These kata are long enough (~50-60 movements) that 1/4 sections contain a substantial number of movements (~13-15 per section). Follow the Torres system directly. All workout formats apply. VLI calculations match the reference manual without modification.

**Category B — Thirds and Halves Only (minimum 1/3):**
Kanku Dai, Sochin, Unsu, Enpi, Gankaku

These kata are shorter (~37-65 movements). A 1/3 section contains roughly 12-22 movements depending on the kata — comparable to or less than a 1/4 section of a Torres-length kata. Default training uses 1/3 sections. Volume (rep count) must be INCREASED to compensate for shorter section length. See Sections 3 and 4 for specifics.

**NOTE on Kanku Dai:** At ~65 movements, Kanku Dai is an exception within Category B — its 1/3 sections (~22 movements) are actually longer than a typical Torres 1/4 section. Kanku Dai does NOT need the volume increase that other Category B kata require. Treat Kanku Dai like a Category A kata for VLI purposes, using 1/3 sections at Torres-standard rep counts.

**Category C — Halves Only (minimum 1/2):**
All Heian kata

Only half-kata work is possible. Each section contains only ~10-14 movements — significantly less work per rep than any Torres section. Volume must be increased substantially. These kata are best used for warm-up/review and EMOM-style workouts, though they can serve volume-building phases with the correct rep adjustments.

## 3. VLI ADJUSTMENTS FOR SHORTER KATA

### The Core Principle

The Torres system's VLI targets assume kata sections of approximately 15 movements (a 1/4 section of a ~60-movement Okinawan kata). When Shotokan sections contain fewer movements, each rep represents less physical work. To achieve equivalent training stimulus, the rep count must increase proportionally.

**What does NOT change across categories:**
- Load targets per phase (same across all categories)
- Rest periods (30 seconds between reps — Torres standard for all)
- Intensity ratings (section size relative to the SAME kata still determines within-kata intensity progression)
- The periodization structure and taper pattern

**What DOES change for shorter kata:**
- Volume (rep count) — INCREASES to compensate for less work per rep

### Volume Adjustment

**Rule of thumb: Increase rep count by ~25% for Category B kata (except Kanku Dai) and by ~50% for Category C kata compared to Torres quarter-based references.**

| Torres Reference (1/4 sections, ~15 movements) | Category B Adaptation (1/3 sections, ~12-16 movements) | Category C Adaptation (1/2 sections, ~10-14 movements) |
|---|---|---|
| ×10 reps per section | ×12-13 reps per section | ×15 reps per section |
| ×5 reps per section | ×6-7 reps per section | ×7-8 reps per section |
| ×3 reps per section | ×4 reps per section | ×5 reps per section |

### Load Calculation

Load = full kata equivalents. The calculation method stays the same. Because Category B kata are shorter overall, one "full kata equivalent" of Sochin represents less physical work than one of Gojushiho Sho. The increased rep counts naturally push load numbers up, which is correct — it compensates for the lower work per equivalent.

| Section Format | Reps per Section | Load Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| 4 quarters × 5 reps | 20 total reps | Load = 5 |
| 3 thirds × 5 reps | 15 total reps | Load = 5 |
| 3 thirds × 7 reps | 21 total reps | Load = 7 |
| 2 halves × 5 reps | 10 total reps | Load = 5 |

**Maximum load ceiling (10 per session) still applies as a safety limit across all categories.**

### Intensity Stays the Same

Unlike the previous version of this document, there is NO baseline intensity shift for Category B or C kata. A 1/3 section of a shorter kata is not more intense than a 1/4 section of a longer kata — the absolute movement count is comparable or lower. Therefore:

- **Rest periods:** 30 seconds between reps for all categories (Torres standard). No extra rest needed.
- **Intensity rating:** Determined by the mix of section sizes relative to that specific kata (1/3 is lower intensity than 1/2 of the SAME kata) and rest periods. Not affected by which category the kata belongs to.

### VLI Targets by Category and Phase

**Category A Kata (Gojushiho Sho/Dai, Kanku Sho) — follows Torres system directly:**

| Phase | Volume Target | Load Target | Intensity | Section Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Loading (6 weeks out) | 28-40 | 7-10 | 2/5 | 1/4 |
| Building (5 weeks) | 20-26 | 6-8 | 3-4/5 | 1/4 + 1/2 |
| Transmutation (3-4 weeks) | 16-20 | 5-7 | 3.5-4/5 | 1/3 + 1/2 |
| Taper (2 weeks) | 10-14 | 4-5 | 4.5/5 | 1/3 + full |
| Competition week | 4-6 | 2 | 5/5 | Full kata |

**Category B Kata (Sochin, Unsu, Enpi, Gankaku) — increased volume, same load targets:**

| Phase | Volume Target | Load Target | Intensity | Section Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Loading (6 weeks out) | 36-50 | 7-10 | 2/5 | 1/3 |
| Building (5 weeks) | 25-33 | 6-8 | 3-4/5 | 1/3 + 1/2 |
| Transmutation (3-4 weeks) | 20-25 | 5-7 | 3.5-4/5 | 1/3 + 1/2 |
| Taper (2 weeks) | 13-18 | 4-5 | 4.5/5 | 1/2 + full |
| Competition week | 5-8 | 2 | 5/5 | Full kata |

**NOTE:** Kanku Dai follows Category A targets despite being Category B for splitting purposes — its 1/3 sections are long enough (~22 movements) to not require the volume increase.

**Category C Kata (Heian series) — Youth Training Only:**

| Phase | Volume Target | Load Target | Intensity | Section Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard training | 10-15 | 5-8 | 2-3/5 | 1/2 |
| Pre-competition | 6-10 | 3-5 | 4/5 | 1/2 + full |
| Competition week | 4-6 | 2 | 5/5 | Full kata |

## 4. WORKOUT FORMAT ADAPTATIONS BY CATEGORY

### Quarter Kata Workout (B1: 50-100-50-100-100)

**Category A:** Use as designed. 1/4 section, 5 reps, no rest between.

**Category B adaptation — "Third Kata Workout":**
```
Pick 1/3 section of kata
6 reps: 50% → 100% → 50% → 100% → 50% → 100%
No rest between reps
2 min rest between rounds
1-3 rounds per kata, 3 katas
```
Rationale: 1/3 sections of shorter kata contain fewer movements than Torres 1/4 sections, so 6 reps (instead of 5) compensates for the shorter section length. The added rep maintains equivalent total work.

**Category C adaptation — "Half Kata Workout":**
```
1/2 section of kata
5 reps: 50% → 100% → 50% → 100% → 100%
No rest between reps
2 min rest between rounds
1-3 rounds per kata, 2-3 katas
```
Rationale: Follows the original Torres pattern. Half sections of Heian kata (~10-14 movements) are shorter than Torres quarter sections, so the standard 5-rep pattern is appropriate.

### EMOM Workout (B2)

**All categories:** Works as designed with 60-second intervals. Category B and C sections are shorter than Torres quarter sections, meaning athletes get MORE rest within the minute, not less. No interval adjustment needed.

| Category | Section Size for EMOM | Interval | Expected Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1/4 or smaller | 60 seconds | ~25-30 sec |
| B | 1/3 | 60 seconds | ~25-35 sec |
| C | 1/2 | 60 seconds | ~25-35 sec |

For all categories, increase rounds by ~25% for Category B and ~50% for Category C to match total training effect (e.g., 10 rounds becomes 12-13 for Category B, 15 for Category C).

### Beginning Blast (B3)

**All categories:** Works as designed. Use 1/4 or 1/3 beginning section depending on kata category. Time-boxed format (8 minutes) self-regulates regardless of section size. For Category B and C, the athlete will naturally fit more reps into 8 minutes due to shorter sections — this is correct and desirable.

### Burpee Kata Endings (A1)

**Category A:** Use last 1/4 as designed.

**Category B adaptation:** Use last 1/3. Keep 5 burpees per set (sections are shorter, not longer — no need to reduce).
```
5 burpees → last 1/3 → 10 sec rest
5 burpees → last 1/3 → 15 sec rest
5 burpees → last 1/3 → 20 sec rest
5 burpees → last 1/3
= 4 reps, 2 min rest between katas, 3 katas
```
For additional volume: add a 5th rep (5 burpees → last 1/3, no rest) to compensate for shorter section length.

**Category C adaptation:** Use last 1/2. Keep 5 burpees, 4-5 reps total.

### Leg Day Kata (A2)

**Category A:** Can use the full 6-line format with 3 sections at 1/4 size grouped into thirds, OR use natural 1/3 splits directly.

**Category B:** Use natural 1/3 splits. Works exactly as designed since the workout is built around thirds. For shorter kata (Enpi, Sochin), consider adding an extra line per focus section (8 lines total instead of 6) to increase volume.

**Category C:** Use 2 halves. Modify format:
```
Line 1: H1@50% → H2@100% → 30s rest
Line 2: H1@50% → H2@100% → 30s rest
Line 3: H1@100% → H2@50% → 30s rest
Line 4: H1@100% → H2@50% → 30s rest
Line 5: H1@50% → H2@100% → 30s rest
Line 6: H1@100% → H2@50%
= 6 lines (expanded from 4 to match training effect), 3 min rest between katas
```

### Upper Body Kata (A3)

**All categories:** Works as designed. Pick any 1/3 section (or 1/2 for Category C). The 5-rep alternation (full-upper-full-upper-full) is independent of section size. For Category B/C, consider adding 1-2 extra reps (7 reps: full-upper-full-upper-full-upper-full) to compensate for shorter sections.

### Circuit Kata Training (EP.5)

**Category A:** Works as designed — 4 stations, 4 sections (quarters).

**Category B adaptation — 3-Station Circuit:**
```
STATION 1: Battle rope × 30 sec → 30 sec rest → Section 1/3 at full power → 30 sec rest
STATION 2: Medicine ball slams × 30 sec → 30 sec rest → Section 2/3 at full power → 30 sec rest
STATION 3: Resistance band knees × 30 sec → 30 sec rest → Section 3/3 at full power
FULL RECOVERY: 2-3 min

Alternative: Keep 4 stations but split one third into two sub-sections for stations 3 and 4
```
Add 1 extra round compared to Category A to compensate for fewer stations per round (e.g., 6 rounds instead of 5 in loading phase).

**Category C adaptation — 2-Station Circuit:**
```
STATION 1: Exercise × 30 sec → 30 sec rest → Half 1 at full power → 30 sec rest
STATION 2: Exercise × 30 sec → 30 sec rest → Half 2 at full power
FULL RECOVERY: 2-3 min
```
Double the cycles per round (2 cycles = 1 effective round) and add extra rounds to match training effect.

### Weight Vest Training (EP.6)

**Category A:** Works as designed — pick 2 quarter sections.

**Category B adaptation:** Pick 2 of the 3 third sections.
```
3× with vest (no rest) → remove vest → 2× without vest (no rest) → 1 min rest
× 3 cycles → 5 min rest
Then: Full kata × 2 with vest → remove → Full kata × 1 without
Do for 2 katas
```
Standard duration (20-40 min). No reduction needed — sections are shorter, so each cycle is less demanding than the Torres original.

**Category C:** Can be used for half-kata sections with the standard protocol. Sections are short enough that quality can be maintained with added weight. Follow the same structure as Category B but with 2 half-sections instead of 2 third-sections.

## 5. STRATEGIC KATA SELECTION FOR SHOTOKAN WKF COMPETITION

### Recommended Competition Repertoire Building

When building a 5-kata competition repertoire for Shotokan, prioritize:

**At least 2 Category A kata** (full splitting flexibility for training):
- Gojushiho Sho (13% of male WKF kata performances — proven competitor)
- Gojushiho Dai
- Kanku Sho

**2-3 Category B kata** (for competitive variety and scoring potential):
- Unsu (13% of male performances, acrobatic elements score well)
- Kanku Dai (longest Shotokan kata, impressive when executed powerfully)
- Sochin (unique character, strong power display)
- Gankaku (distinctive one-leg stances, balance showcase)
- Enpi (speed showcase, but shortest — limited training utility)

**Training efficiency consideration:** Having more Category A kata in the repertoire makes the Torres VLI system easier to apply directly. A repertoire heavy on Category B kata simply requires higher rep counts to achieve equivalent training stimulus.

### Ideal 5-Kata Portfolio Examples

**Male U21/Senior — Power-focused:**
1. Unsu (Category B — signature kata, acrobatic scoring)
2. Gojushiho Sho (Category A — precision showcase)
3. Kanku Dai (Category B — length and power)
4. Sochin (Category B — rooted power, unique character)
5. Gojushiho Dai (Category A — versatility)

**Male Cadet/Junior — Development-focused:**
1. Kanku Sho (Category A — accessible difficulty, full training utility)
2. Gojushiho Sho (Category A — builds toward elite repertoire)
3. Gankaku (Category B — balance and precision)
4. Enpi (Category B — speed and transitions)
5. Kanku Dai (Category B — endurance and power development)

**Female — Precision and Expression:**
1. Gojushiho Dai (Category A — expression and rhythm)
2. Unsu (Category B — athletic impression)
3. Kanku Sho (Category A — clean technique showcase)
4. Gojushiho Sho (Category A — precision)
5. Sochin or Kanku Dai (Category B — power contrast)

## 6. COACH NOTES: NATURAL SPLITTING POINTS

When defining where to split each kata into sections, align with natural break points:

**Natural splitting markers (in order of preference):**
1. Kiai points (primary natural breaks)
2. Major directional changes (180° turns, new embusen line)
3. Tempo/rhythm changes (transition from fast to slow or vice versa)
4. Thematic movement clusters (a series of related techniques that form a unit)

**AVOID splitting:**
- In the middle of a combination (e.g., between a block and its counter-strike)
- Immediately before or after a balance-critical technique
- At points where the next technique requires momentum from the previous one

The coach should define and DOCUMENT the exact split points for each kata in each athlete's profile. This ensures consistency across sessions and allows Claude to reference specific sections when generating training plans.
